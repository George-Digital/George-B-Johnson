# Assemble the first context pack

**A first context pack is a small, current, traceable set of sources, rules, examples, decision records, access boundaries, and review routes that supports one bounded workflow.**

I assemble the first pack by selecting the approved material for the common path and making its authority, location, owners, limits, and human-review points easy to inspect.

Assembly is where I resist the urge to turn a useful first system into a repository of everything. I return to the use case: who is the operator, what task are they performing, what output are they preparing or reviewing, and what sources do they need for that job? The pack should make that common path easier. If a document does not support a known question, rule, output field, or escalation route, I leave it in the inventory or candidate list rather than adding it to active context.

I start with a short overview. It says what the pack is for, the intended user, the current scope, the source-of-truth locations, and the human-review boundary. I include a version date and the pack owner. A new operator should understand in a minute that this is a working view for a limited purpose, not a general authorization to answer every question in the organization.

Then I add the source map. The map lists the approved facts, rule sources, examples, and decision records that may be relevant, along with their owners, authority statuses, access limits, and review dates. I use references to approved locations instead of copying restricted material into the pack. The reference is specific enough for a permitted reviewer to trace an important claim, but it does not expose information to people who should not receive it.

I organize the active content by function. Current case facts or approved fact references belong together. Operating rules and their authority sources belong together. Examples are plainly labeled as examples and separated from live case material. Decisions and exceptions carry the decision maker, scope, date, and expiration. A Review Needed section makes missing, conflicting, pending, or access-limited context visible before the operator produces a final-looking draft.

I include only the rules needed for the common path and known pauses. A rule should say what triggers it, what action follows, what evidence is required, and who owns the exception. I do not turn a past preference into a rule or let an AI-generated summary replace the approved source. When a rule affects a high-impact area such as health, law, finance, employment, safety, eligibility, discipline, or access, the pack makes clear that AI assistance remains advisory and a qualified human retains final judgment.

I add the intake process and maintenance policy near the pack, even if they live as linked templates. An operator needs to know how a new source enters, what status it has while pending, who can approve a change, and what happens when it expires. This turns the pack from a static snapshot into a maintainable system. I also include the access matrix or a safe reference to it, so the user knows who may view, run, edit, approve, and share without treating a link as blanket permission.

The pack needs a retrieval path. I write a few ordinary workflow questions and show where the operator starts: “What source controls this deadline?” “What information is required before I draft?” “How should I label an unknown?” “Who resolves a conflict?” The pack can contain direct answers for the common path or links to approved sources. It should not require the operator to scan every file or rely on memory.

Before calling the pack ready for testing, I do a human walkthrough. I ask a capable reviewer to locate the current rule, trace one important fact, distinguish an example from a rule, find the access boundary, and identify the stop route. I use an authorized or synthetic case. If the reviewer cannot complete one of those tasks, I improve the labels, source map, or retrieval cue before blaming the model or adding more documents.

I preserve the assembly decisions in the build log: why each source entered the active pack, why a candidate stayed out, what access limitation applies, and what question still needs owner review. That record lets me revise responsibly after grounding and retrieval tests. The first pack is complete enough to test when a reviewer can inspect its purpose, approved context, and boundaries—not when it looks exhaustive.

## Concrete example

A project coordinator assembles a kickoff-brief pack with a one-page overview, source map, current project facts, approved kickoff rules, a sanitized example brief, decision records for temporary exceptions, an intake link, and a Review Needed route. The signed scope remains in its approved location and is referenced from the pack. The coordinator can prepare a draft; the project lead verifies scope claims and resolves any conflict before the brief is sent.

## Action checklist

- Add a one-page purpose, scope, version, owner, and human-review overview.
- Include only approved common-path sources from the inventory and map their authority.
- Separate fact references, rules, examples, decisions, and review-needed items.
- Link to approved source-of-truth locations instead of copying restricted content.
- Add intake, maintenance, access, missing-context, and conflict routes.
- Walk through the pack with a human reviewer using a safe case.

## Required deliverable

Assemble **Context Pack Version 1** with an overview, source map, active source references, classification labels, authority rules, access boundary, intake route, maintenance policy, missing-context playbook, human-review point, and a recorded walkthrough finding.
