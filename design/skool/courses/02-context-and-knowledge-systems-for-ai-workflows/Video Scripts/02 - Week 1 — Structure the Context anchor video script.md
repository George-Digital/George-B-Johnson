# Week 1: Make source authority and uncertainty visible

**Embed on:** `Week 1 — Structure the Context/01 - Separate facts, rules, examples, and decisions.md`

**Estimated recording time:** 9–11 minutes

**Outcome:** Members will structure their active context around two facts, two rules, one example, and one decision; write a complete authority/traceability rule for an important claim; and create three source-intake records with distinct, reviewable outcomes.

## What to demonstrate on screen

Use only the **Northstar Neighborhood Center volunteer shift-brief workflow — SYNTHETIC TRAINING EXAMPLE.** Use fictional titles and roles; do not display real documents, source text, personal data, credentials, or confidential decision records.

1. Open `Resources/Context Inventory and Source Map Template.md` and show the completed synthetic rows from the Start Here demo, including classification, authority status, source owner, approved access boundary, retrieval cue, and traceability method.
2. Create a simple synthetic four-column working view: `Current Facts`, `Operating Rules`, `Examples Only`, and `Decisions and Exceptions`. Place safe references—not copied source contents—under each column, and keep these six labeled items visible:
   - `Fact 1:` Current shift starts at `09:00` — `Northstar Event Schedule v2.1 — synthetic`.
   - `Fact 2:` Approved coordinator check-in uses the recorded venue entry and check-in point — `Northstar Venue Access Guide v1.3 — synthetic`.
   - `Rule 1:` Volunteer safety handling follows `Volunteer Safety Procedure v4 — synthetic`.
   - `Rule 2:` Weather changes follow `Severe Weather Escalation Procedure v2 — synthetic`.
   - `Example:` Brief format only — `Prior Shift Brief — synthetic example only`.
   - `Decision:` One-time rain-plan change; event lead; `2026-09-12`; named event scope and expiry/reconsideration point.
3. Show the complete authority/traceability rule for the `09:00` shift-time claim: the workflow question/output field, `Northstar Event Schedule v2.1 — synthetic` as controlling source, its event-lead owner, stated current-shift scope, review date, approved coordinator access, retrieval cue, traceability method, related conflicting source, and the route to the event lead if the source is absent, stale, conflicting, or restricted.
4. Show a separate traced draft-output claim: `Draft shift brief: “The volunteer shift begins at 09:00.”` Link it to `Northstar Event Schedule v2.1 — synthetic`, the source owner, review date, and its approved retrieval reference. Do not show source text.
5. Show a safe source-authority conflict: `Northstar Event Schedule v2.1 — synthetic` says `09:00`; `Planning Notes v1.8 — synthetic, old` says `08:30`. Mark the schedule as controlling only because its owner, scope, and review date are recorded. If those conditions were absent, label the output claim “Needs event-lead confirmation.”
6. Open `Resources/Context Intake Prompt.md`. Show the headings for proposed classification, provenance captured, authority assessment, access assessment, overlap or conflict, status, active-pack recommendation, missing information, and human-review requirement.
7. Create and leave visible all three synthetic intake records, each with provenance, authority, access, overlap/conflict, status, active-pack disposition, unanswered questions, and human-review route. Give each a different outcome:
   - `Northstar Venue Access Guide v1.3 — synthetic` → `Accepted after venue-lead review` → `Include`.
   - `Volunteer Orientation Notes — synthetic, owner unknown` → `Pending owner review` → `Candidate list` and not active retrieval.
   - `Planning Notes v1.8 — synthetic, old` → `Rejected` → `Exclude`; retain only the conflict reference in the build log, not as support for a claim.

## Teleprompter script

[ON SCREEN: Title card: “Week 1 — Structure the Context”]

Hi, I’m George. This week we turn an inventory into a context structure that tells people what each item means and what it is allowed to support.

[ON SCREEN: Four-column view: Current Facts / Operating Rules / Examples Only / Decisions and Exceptions]

A context pack becomes risky when every document looks equally true. A case schedule, a procedure, a previous output, and a manager’s exception may all be useful. But they are not interchangeable. I want the structure to answer a simple question before anyone drafts: is this a fact, a rule, an example, or a decision?

[ON SCREEN: Synthetic Northstar source-map rows in `Resources/Context Inventory and Source Map Template.md`]

I’m continuing with the fictional Northstar volunteer shift-brief workflow. This is synthetic training material only. The completed inventory keeps source references, owners, current status, access boundaries, retrieval cues, and traceability methods together so a reviewer does not have to trust a neat sentence on its own.

[ON SCREEN: Add `Fact 1` and `Fact 2` under Current Facts]

First, I place two current facts in the working view. Fact one is that this synthetic shift begins at 09:00, supported by Northstar Event Schedule v2.1. Fact two is the approved coordinator check-in entry and point, supported by Northstar Venue Access Guide v1.3. These are separate claims with separate sources. A schedule does not prove an access detail, and a venue guide does not prove a shift time.

[ON SCREEN: Add `Rule 1` and `Rule 2` under Operating Rules]

Next, I place two operating rules under their own heading. Rule one points to Volunteer Safety Procedure v4 for safety handling. Rule two points to Severe Weather Escalation Procedure v2 for a weather change. Rules tell the coordinator what to do when a condition occurs. They need an accountable owner and current status; a past brief cannot override them because it happens to be readable.

[ON SCREEN: Add the example and the dated decision under their separate headings]

The Prior Shift Brief is one example only. It can show the format for a clear brief, but it cannot supply a fact for today. The one-time rain-plan change is a decision, not a general rule. I record the event lead, the date, the named event scope, and the point at which it expires or needs reconsideration. AI may summarize evidence or draft options, but it does not own that decision.

[ON SCREEN: Complete authority/traceability rule for the shift-time claim]

Now I write the full authority and traceability rule for one important workflow question: what time does this shift begin? The controlling source is Event Schedule v2.1 because the event lead owns it, its scope is the current shift time, and its review date is known. I also record who may access it, the retrieval cue, and how a permitted reviewer can trace the claim back to the approved schedule reference.

The rule is not complete until it names the failure route. Planning Notes v1.8 is a related older source that conflicts with the schedule. If the schedule is absent, stale, restricted, or no longer has a confirmed owner, the system does not select a time. It marks the claim “Needs event-lead confirmation” and routes the question to the event lead.

[ON SCREEN: Traced draft output claim: “The volunteer shift begins at 09:00.” with source, owner, review date, and approved retrieval reference]

Here is the result in a draft output. I write: “The volunteer shift begins at 09:00.” Beside that claim, I show Event Schedule v2.1, the event-lead owner, its review date, and the approved retrieval reference. That is a traced output claim: an authorized reviewer can inspect why this sentence is here without seeing restricted source content in the shared pack.

[ON SCREEN: Synthetic conflict card: Schedule v2.1 09:00 vs Planning Notes v1.8 08:30]

Let’s test that authority rule. The current schedule says 09:00, and old planning notes say 08:30. We do not choose the answer that is clearer, longer, or more convenient. In this synthetic case, the schedule controls because the owner, scope, and review date are recorded. The old notes document a conflict, but they are not evidence an operator may use to set the time.

[ON SCREEN: Replace schedule status with “unknown” and display “Needs event-lead confirmation”]

Authority is not magic. If the schedule had no confirmed owner or no known current status, I would not pretend the conflict is resolved. The correct draft would say, “Shift time needs event-lead confirmation,” preserve safe references for the permitted reviewer, and stop distribution until that person decides. A missing answer is sometimes the most responsible outcome.

[ON SCREEN: Open `Resources/Context Intake Prompt.md`]

This is why intake matters. New content does not become active just because someone sends it or because it contains a convincing instruction. This prompt creates a draft intake record: where the material came from, what it might support, its proposed classification, authority and access assessment, overlap, status, and the human reviewer.

[ON SCREEN: Intake record 1 — `Northstar Venue Access Guide v1.3 — synthetic`; Accepted after venue-lead review; Include]

The first of three intake records is the Venue Access Guide. Its venue-lead owner, date, scope, and approved coordinator access are confirmed by the proper reviewer. Its outcome is Accepted after venue-lead review, with an Include disposition. Accepted means the evidence and review support this limited use; it does not mean the source can answer unrelated questions.

[ON SCREEN: Intake record 2 — `Volunteer Orientation Notes — synthetic, owner unknown`; Pending owner review; Candidate list]

The second record is Volunteer Orientation Notes with an unknown owner. It may be useful, but that is not enough. I mark it Pending owner review and keep it on the Candidate list, not active retrieval. The record names the missing provenance, authority, date, and access confirmation that the owner must resolve.

[ON SCREEN: Intake record 3 — `Planning Notes v1.8 — synthetic, old`; Rejected; Exclude]

The third record is the old Planning Notes. Its 08:30 claim conflicts with the current schedule, and it does not meet the current authority requirement for this workflow. Its outcome is Rejected and Exclude. I retain only a safe reference to the conflict in the build log; I do not let a rejected source support a draft claim.

[ON SCREEN: Four structure questions]

A usable structure lets an operator find two current facts, the two rules that apply, the example that illustrates format, and the human who owns a decision or exception. It also makes the “I don’t know yet” route obvious.

[ON SCREEN: Close-up of safe references rather than source text]

Keep restricted sources in their approved systems. A context pack can use a safe reference and the appropriate access route. A label is useful, but it does not grant permission. If access is unclear, pause and route the question; do not copy private information into a shared pack or prompt to make retrieval easier.

[ON SCREEN: Week 1 action list]

This week, classify your material, write one complete authority and traceability rule, and test it with a traced draft claim. Then create three intake records that show the difference between an accepted source, a pending source, and a rejected source. The goal is not an elaborate taxonomy. The goal is a working structure that makes claims traceable, uncertainty visible, and human authority clear.

## Member action

1. Complete `Week 1 — Structure the Context/01 - Separate facts, rules, examples, and decisions.md` by labeling **two facts, two rules, one example, and one decision or exception**. For each, include a safe source reference, owner, scope, and any access limit.
2. Write one **complete authority/traceability rule** for an important workflow question or output field. It must state the question or claim; controlling source identifier; why it controls (authority and scope); owner; current, effective, or review date/status; approved access boundary; approved location or retrieval cue; traceability method; related or conflicting sources and their permitted use; and the named route if the source is absent, stale, conflicting, or restricted.
3. Draft one output claim supported by that rule. Attach its source identifier, owner, date/status, and approved retrieval reference or traceability method so a permitted reviewer can verify the claim without copying restricted content.
4. Use `Resources/Context Intake Prompt.md` with only authorized, minimized, or synthetic material to create **all three** draft intake records: one `Accepted` / `Include` record after the required owner review, one `Pending owner review` / `Candidate list` record, and one `Rejected` / `Exclude` record. Each record must include provenance, authority assessment, access assessment, overlap or conflict, status, active-pack disposition, missing-information questions, and a human-review next action.
5. Record one conflict or unknown-status case in the build log. Preserve safe references and route it to the designated owner; do not resolve it by guesswork.
