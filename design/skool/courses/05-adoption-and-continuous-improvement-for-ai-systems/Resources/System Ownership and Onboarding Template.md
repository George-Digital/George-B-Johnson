## Applied prompt: turn operating notes into an onboarding guide

**Use case:** You have a working process and need a plain-language first-use guide for one operator role.

**Inputs:** System definition; user role; common-path steps; approved input location; output; human-review point; exclusions; support route; privacy boundary; one safe example.

**Paste-ready prompt:**

> Act as a careful operations writer. Draft a one-page onboarding guide for the stated operator role. Open with one sentence that says what the system helps with, when to use it, and what it does not decide. Then write the common path as short observable actions. Include the approved input location, the data users must not enter, the required human-review step, one missing-information example, the routine support route, the stop or escalation route, and the manual fallback. Do not invent policies, permissions, facts, or authority. Mark any missing input as “Owner must confirm.” Use plain language that a first-time operator can follow.

**Expected output:** A compact orientation, common-path instructions, boundaries, and support routes that can be reviewed by the operating owner.

**Failure mode:** The draft may sound complete while omitting a local permission, accessibility need, or real exception. It may also oversimplify a boundary that belongs to a policy or privacy owner.

**Human review:** The operating owner verifies fit with real work; the source, privacy, access, or domain owner verifies any instruction that affects their authority. Test the guide with a safe first-use walkthrough before relying on it.
