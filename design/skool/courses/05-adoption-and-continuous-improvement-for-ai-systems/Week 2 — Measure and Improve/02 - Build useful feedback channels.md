# Build useful feedback channels

**Feedback improves a system when it reaches the right owner with enough context to support a specific decision, not when it becomes a pile of opinions.**

I build feedback channels around the moments when people notice something useful: after a first run, after a reviewer corrects a draft, when a recipient cannot act on an output, or when an operator chooses the manual fallback. I do not ask users to remember general impressions at the end of a month. I make it easy to capture a short, safe observation close to the work.

Each channel has a purpose. A quick post-use question can reveal friction: Was this the right case for the system? Was the output usable after review? What slowed you down? A reviewer note can identify a specific missing field or unsupported claim. A periodic conversation can surface broader concerns, such as whether the system changes a role boundary or creates pressure to accept drafts. I keep these purposes distinct so an urgent concern is not buried in a satisfaction survey.

I ask for evidence that the owner can act on. “It was bad” is a starting signal, but I need to know the case type, expected behavior, actual behavior, impact, current version, and whether a fallback or escalation occurred. When private information is involved, the feedback form asks for an approved case reference or sanitized description rather than pasted records. I tell users exactly where sensitive evidence belongs and who may view it.

I give feedback a route and a response. Routine observations go into the issue or feedback log. A concern involving privacy, safety, authorization, or a consequential decision uses the escalation route and may require the process to stop. The operating owner acknowledges what they received, classifies it, and brings patterns into the scheduled review. I do not promise that every suggestion will become a feature. I do explain the decision: revise, defer, narrow, maintain, or stop.

I seek feedback from more than the most vocal early adopter. I ask the new user, the experienced operator, the reviewer, and where appropriate the recipient of the handoff. Their perspectives can conflict, and that is useful. A manager may value a concise draft while the operator needs clearer source references. I look for an operating design that lets both do their part rather than selecting the easiest opinion.

I make participation safe. Users should be able to report uncertainty, a mistake, or an awkward workaround without being blamed for not embracing the system. I explicitly invite reports of cases that did not fit. I also make clear that feedback is not a channel for entering confidential personnel, client, or health information unless the route is approved for it.

Finally, I close the loop. At the review cadence, I summarize patterns and decisions in plain language: what we heard, what changed, what did not change, why, and what users should do next. That visible response turns feedback into an operating habit rather than a request that disappears into a form.

## Concrete example

For a grant-application briefing workflow, I add a two-question post-use form: “Could you complete the next review step with this draft?” and “What required information was unclear, missing, or unsafe to include?” Reviewers can add a sanitized note tied to the application reference. A privacy concern goes directly to the authorized route, not into the form. In the weekly review, the owner sees that several users want longer summaries, but reviewers repeatedly need clearer source references. The next revision adds source labels before expanding length.

## Action checklist

- Identify the work moments where a user, reviewer, or recipient can give useful feedback.
- Give each channel a narrow purpose and an appropriate owner.
- Ask for expected behavior, actual behavior, impact, version, and fallback or escalation status.
- Provide a secure route for sensitive evidence and a safe route for general observations.
- Include reports from users with different roles and levels of experience.
- Publish a plain-language response to recurring feedback and decisions.

## Required deliverable

Create an **Adoption Feedback Plan** with post-use questions, reviewer feedback fields, periodic discussion prompts, privacy and escalation routes, response ownership, issue classification, review timing, and a method for closing the loop with users.
