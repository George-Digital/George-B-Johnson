# Set the review cadence

**A review cadence turns scattered operating evidence into timely decisions before small failures become normal practice.**

I set a review cadence that matches the pace and consequence of the system. A high-volume workflow may need a short weekly check on a sample of outputs. A low-volume process may need a review after each use or each material case. A workflow that affects safety, privacy, legal, financial, employment, or access decisions needs the cadence and qualified review appropriate to its governing obligations. I do not borrow a calendar rhythm just because it sounds disciplined.

I give each review a clear purpose. An early pilot review asks whether users can follow the common path, whether the support route works, and whether any boundary requires a pause. An operating review looks at usage, quality, fallbacks, feedback, source changes, and open issues. A deeper periodic review asks whether the system still serves its purpose, whether ownership and permissions remain correct, and whether it should be maintained, narrowed, paused, or retired.

I keep the agenda small enough to finish. I start with stop-level concerns and unresolved escalations. Then I inspect a representative set of outputs or quality records, the scorecard, feedback patterns, source or dependency changes, and proposed changes. I finish by recording a decision, owner, due point, and what users need to know. A meeting without a recorded decision can become an interesting conversation that leaves the process unchanged.

I make attendance role-based. The operating owner brings evidence and coordinates. The reviewer or domain lead interprets quality where judgment is required. The source owner confirms relevant reference changes. A privacy, access, or technical owner participates when their boundary is affected. Not every role needs every meeting, but the people making a decision must have authority and enough context. I avoid inviting a large group merely to create a sense of governance.

I prepare evidence before the review. I use a safe summary, approved references, and aggregate patterns where possible. I do not circulate raw personal records to a broad group to make the discussion concrete. If an incident requires restricted handling, it follows the authorized escalation process, with only the resulting operating decision recorded in the shared log.

I also define what happens between reviews. A user should not wait for the monthly meeting if a current source is invalid or a critical quality check fails. The SOP and support plan state when the owner may make a routine correction, when they must consult another role, and when use pauses. The cadence is a routine for learning, not permission to ignore an immediate concern.

Over time, I revisit the cadence itself. If the workflow is stable, low risk, and supported by evidence, a lighter routine may be appropriate. If use expands, sources become volatile, or review uncovers recurring failure, I increase attention. The right cadence is the one that keeps the operating decision close enough to the evidence.

## Concrete example

For a monthly board-report drafting workflow, I schedule a fifteen-minute weekly pilot check with the operating owner and finance reviewer, plus a monthly source and access review with the policy owner. The weekly check reviews a sample of drafts, manual fallbacks, and unresolved questions. If a draft presents an unsupported financial claim, the owner stops use for that report section and routes it to finance immediately rather than waiting for the next meeting. The shared record keeps only the safe issue summary, decision, owner, and next step.

## Action checklist

- Match early, operating, and periodic reviews to volume, consequence, and source volatility.
- Define the purpose and decision for each review type.
- Start with stop-level concerns, then inspect evidence and patterns.
- Invite only roles with relevant authority or operating knowledge.
- Prepare safe summaries and approved references before the meeting.
- Record decisions, owners, next steps, and user communication after every review.

## Required deliverable

Create a **Review Cadence Plan** with meeting or review types, triggers, participants, evidence inputs, agenda, decision options, between-review stop rules, record location, and the next scheduled review dates or event triggers.
