# Define pause and retirement criteria

**A responsible system has clear conditions for stopping or retiring use when its evidence, ownership, quality, or permissions no longer support the work.**

I define pause and retirement criteria before a system becomes inconvenient to question. Teams are often willing to add users and features, but less willing to state what would make the workflow inappropriate to continue. Writing those conditions protects users from silent drift and gives the operating owner permission to act when a boundary is crossed.

A pause is a temporary stop for all or part of the workflow while we investigate, correct, or obtain the needed approval. I use it when a source becomes unreliable, a material dependency changes, a critical quality check fails, an access or privacy concern appears, review capacity disappears, or a case type proves outside scope. The pause can be narrow. I may stop one output section or one user group while a well-supported part of the process continues. The key is that users know what has stopped and what manual route replaces it.

Retirement is a more deliberate decision to end the system or replace it with another approved process. I consider it when the original problem no longer exists, usage remains low because the workflow does not fit, the manual path is clearer, source maintenance is not sustainable, risk exceeds the evidence or review capacity, a better authorized process replaces it, or the system has accumulated patches without a coherent operating model. Retirement is not a failure. It can be the clearest outcome of honest evidence.

I make criteria observable. “If people do not like it” is not enough. I might define repeated critical omissions, a recurring inability to verify sources, a fixed period without an accountable owner, unresolved privacy or access concerns, sustained reliance on manual fallback, or a source owner’s decision that the workflow no longer has an approved basis. I include qualitative evidence too, especially from people affected by the handoff. A pattern of confusion or pressure to accept drafts can matter even if raw usage is high.

I assign authority. The operating owner may pause a routine path within their scope. A source, privacy, legal, safety, or executive owner may be needed for a material decision. I identify who communicates the pause, who updates the SOP and onboarding guide, who preserves the safe operating record, and who assists users with the manual fallback. No one should discover a retirement only because an old link stops working.

I plan the review that follows a pause. We inspect the trigger, affected cases, evidence, source and dependency status, user impact, and possible paths: correct and retest, narrow scope, replace the workflow, or retire it. We do not resume merely because a deadline is uncomfortable. Resumption requires the named owner and the agreed test or approval.

I preserve records proportionately at retirement. I keep the current decision, reason, approved references, relevant change history, and any retention or deletion requirement. I remove access where appropriate and avoid retaining private material beyond its authorized purpose. I also tell users where the work now belongs. A clean ending is part of responsible maintenance.

## Concrete example

A sales-operations team uses a workflow to draft account handoff notes from approved CRM fields. After a CRM permission update, the workflow begins receiving incomplete account data, and reviewers find several drafts suggesting next steps without an authorized owner. The operating owner pauses the automated draft for affected accounts and directs users to the existing manual handoff template. The CRM owner confirms that the field access will not be restored, so the team tests a narrower workflow using only approved fields. If the narrower draft still cannot support the handoff, they retire the workflow, archive the safe change record, remove the outdated instructions, and communicate the manual process to users.

## Action checklist

- Define observable triggers for a temporary pause and for retirement.
- Separate routine quality issues from privacy, access, source, and consequential-boundary concerns.
- Name who can pause, investigate, approve resumption, and authorize retirement.
- Specify the manual fallback and user communication for every pause.
- Define the evidence and test required before resumption.
- Plan records, access changes, and the replacement process for retirement.

## Required deliverable

Create **Pause and Retirement Criteria** with trigger categories, severity or scope guidance, authority roles, immediate stop action, manual fallback, investigation steps, resumption requirements, retirement decision process, communications, and approved record-retention or access-closeout actions.
