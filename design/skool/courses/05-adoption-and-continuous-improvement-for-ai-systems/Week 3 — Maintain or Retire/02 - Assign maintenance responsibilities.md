# Assign maintenance responsibilities

**Maintenance works when routine care, source updates, quality review, support, and retirement decisions have named owners instead of assumed volunteers.**

I treat maintenance as part of the system, not as work someone will do after the exciting launch. An AI-assisted workflow can remain small and still require care: someone confirms the current instructions, someone watches the source material, someone reviews quality, someone handles support, and someone can decide to pause or retire it. Naming those responsibilities protects both users and the system owner.

I start by listing the recurring maintenance work. There is often an operating check: is the approved version available, are users following the common path, and are fallbacks being recorded? There is source care: are policies, knowledge documents, forms, or data fields current? There is quality care: are reviewers seeing the same failure pattern or boundary issue? There is technical care where a tool, connector, access group, or configuration can change. There is also communication: who tells users when a process changes or stops?

I assign a single accountable owner for each responsibility, even when several people contribute. Shared responsibility can be valuable; unnamed responsibility creates delay. I write the decision that the owner may make and the boundary where they must consult someone else. For example, an operating owner may clarify an instruction and schedule a retest, while a policy owner approves a source interpretation and a privacy owner approves a data-boundary change.

I make the workload realistic. A solo operator may hold multiple roles for a small, low-risk system, but they still need a backup or escalation path for absences and conflicts. A team should not assign a weekly review to someone who never sees the output or cannot access the evidence. I match the maintenance task to the person’s authority, knowledge, capacity, and access.

I also plan handoff. People leave, change roles, or go on leave. A maintenance record should say where the current SOP, scorecard, change log, sources, access instructions, and open decisions live. The incoming owner needs a brief orientation and a safe way to see the latest evidence. I avoid making a personal inbox, a private chat, or one person’s memory the only home for an active system.

I define how the owner knows that maintenance is due. It may be a calendar review, a volume threshold, a source update notice, a recurring issue pattern, or a change in scope. I include the time and resources needed. If the team cannot provide the required review, source care, or authorized access, the responsible decision may be to narrow or pause the workflow rather than pretending it remains maintained.

Maintenance responsibility includes the authority to say no. The owner can decline a feature request that expands the workflow beyond its tested purpose, route a new case type to another process, or pause use while a question is resolved. This is not an obstacle to adoption. It preserves a clear, useful system for the people who rely on it.

## Concrete example

A community organization uses a workflow to prepare event-accessibility briefs from approved planning notes. The event coordinator owns daily operation and user questions. The accessibility lead reviews the checklist criteria and any accommodation concern. The shared-services team owns the approved event form and access permissions. A technical volunteer checks the connector after platform updates but cannot change the accessibility rules. The director can pause use if review capacity is unavailable. Their maintenance record names a backup coordinator and stores the current documents in an approved shared workspace, not a personal account.

## Action checklist

- List every recurring operating, source, quality, technical, support, and communication task.
- Assign one accountable owner and any contributing roles for each task.
- State the routine decision authority and escalation boundary for every owner.
- Confirm each owner has access, time, and a backup or handoff route.
- Identify where current documents, evidence, and open decisions live.
- Set the calendar or event trigger that makes maintenance due.

## Required deliverable

Create a **Maintenance Responsibility Map** with each maintenance task, accountable owner, contributors, authority boundary, backup, required access, cadence or trigger, evidence location, and pause or escalation route.
