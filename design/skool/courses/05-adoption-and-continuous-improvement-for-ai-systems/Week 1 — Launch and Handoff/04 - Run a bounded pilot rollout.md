# Run a bounded pilot rollout

**A bounded pilot tests an operating process with real work while limiting scope, protecting people, and preserving a clear fallback.**

I use a pilot to learn whether a system works in the conditions where people actually need it. I do not use it as a ceremonial launch or as proof that the process should spread immediately. The pilot has a named group, a defined use case, a short time window or case limit, an owner, review gates, and a decision at the end.

I set boundaries first. I choose users who face the recurring workflow, can use the approved inputs, and have a way to give candid feedback. I define which cases are included and which remain outside the pilot. I may exclude urgent, high-consequence, highly sensitive, or unusual cases until the process has suitable evidence and approval. Narrowing the pilot protects users from being asked to experiment where the cost of a bad output is too high.

I explain the pilot in practical terms. Participants know the purpose, what will change in their workflow, what is still manual, what signals we will observe, and how to report a problem. I make the human-review boundary visible at every use. I do not present a draft as an authoritative answer, and I do not make participation a substitute for an authorized decision in employment, legal, medical, financial, safety, or access matters.

Before the first case, I confirm that the operating record, onboarding guide, SOP, support route, current sources, and manual fallback are available. I run a safe dry run when possible. I also verify access: only authorized people can reach the input and output locations, and no one has been asked to move private material into an unapproved tool or channel. A pilot that bypasses privacy controls to move faster is not a useful test.

During the pilot, I keep observation lightweight but disciplined. The operator records each use or a representative sample, the result of the review gate, any fallback, and a short note on friction. The owner watches for patterns, not just volume. If output quality drops, sources change, users cannot complete the common path, or a stop-level concern appears, I pause or narrow the pilot. Continuing simply to reach a target number of cases can turn a learning exercise into avoidable risk.

I schedule a midpoint check before the pilot ends. This gives participants a chance to surface confusion while a small change can still be tested. I distinguish a training gap from a system gap. If one person cannot find a field, the guide may need work. If several users cannot obtain the required source, the workflow may not fit its real operating conditions.

At the end, I make a bounded decision: maintain the current version for the defined use, revise and run another pilot, narrow the scope, pause, or retire it. I bring the evidence, limitations, and participant feedback into that decision. The aim is not a verdict that AI is good or bad. It is an honest decision about this system in this context.

## Concrete example

I pilot an AI-assisted weekly project-status brief with four project coordinators for two weeks and no more than twenty updates. It can summarize approved project notes but cannot create commitments, assess personnel performance, or process confidential HR material. Each coordinator reviews the draft with the project lead before distribution and can use the existing manual brief at any time. At the midpoint, two coordinators report that outdated milestones are being repeated. The owner pauses new automated briefs, confirms the source update process, revises the pre-run check, and resumes only after a safe retest.

## Action checklist

- Define the pilot users, included cases, duration or case limit, and exclusions.
- Explain the purpose, changed step, human-review boundary, and fallback to participants.
- Confirm onboarding, SOP, support, source access, and privacy controls before the first use.
- Record use, review result, friction, fallback, and material issue in a safe log.
- Schedule a midpoint review and define pause triggers.
- Choose the end-of-pilot decision options before seeing the results.

## Required deliverable

Create a **Bounded Pilot Plan and Record** with participants, scope, exclusions, consent or communication approach where needed, current operating materials, observation signals, review gates, privacy controls, stop triggers, fallback, midpoint check, and end-of-pilot decision meeting.
