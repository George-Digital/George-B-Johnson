# Establish the operating baseline

**A baseline gives me a truthful before-state so I can judge whether a change in use, quality, burden, or risk is worth keeping.**

I cannot learn much from a pilot if I only collect impressions after launch. Before I change a recurring workflow, I take a simple picture of how the work happens now. I am not trying to build a perfect analytics program. I am trying to preserve enough evidence that the team can distinguish a useful improvement from novelty, a shift in workload, or a problem we failed to see.

I begin with the current path. I observe or reconstruct a few representative cases: what triggered the work, who touched it, what information was missing, how long the common path took, where it waited, and what required rework. I include the manual steps that people may have stopped noticing, such as searching for a source, rewriting an explanation, asking for approval, or checking an exception. These often become the actual adoption burden.

Then I choose a small baseline set. For a weekly process, I might use the previous four weeks. For a high-volume process, I might sample ten or twenty safe cases. For a lower-volume, higher-consequence process, I might document fewer cases in more detail and keep qualified review in place. I avoid collecting more personal data than the purpose requires. When I need examples, I use approved access, anonymized summaries, or synthetic cases rather than copying sensitive material into a shared worksheet.

I record both usage and quality before I make a comparison. Usage can include volume, completion, handoff time, or number of manual fallbacks. Quality can include required-field completeness, correction rate, reviewer concerns, policy adherence, or whether the output supports the next action. I also note the human cost: confusion, interruptions, training time, and extra review. Faster is not automatically better if it shifts risk or creates more downstream work.

I write down the current sources and dependencies too. A workflow may look slow because the approved knowledge base is scattered or because a form has inconsistent fields. If the pilot changes several things at once, I mark that honestly. I do not claim that one prompt caused a result when a new template, a new owner, and a new staffing pattern all changed together.

I establish an observation period that matches the workflow. I want enough cases to see recurring behavior, not a single favorable day. I also name the events that should stop normal measurement: a major policy change, an outage, a sensitive incident, or a change in the user group. In those situations, the owner should record the context and decide whether the baseline comparison remains useful.

A baseline is an operating aid, not a performance weapon. I tell users what is being observed, why, who can see it, and how feedback will be used. I do not use it to rank people or justify a consequential employment decision. The point is to improve a process with the people doing it, while keeping human judgment and privacy boundaries intact.

## Concrete example

A customer-success team currently turns implementation-call notes into follow-up tasks. I sample twelve approved, de-identified call summaries. The baseline shows that 8 of 12 follow-ups require a manager correction, 5 omit an owner, and the coordinator spends about fifteen minutes per summary including source checks. I record that the existing notes vary widely and that urgent account issues already use a separate escalation route. The pilot can now compare its output against those conditions instead of claiming improvement from one polished example.

## Action checklist

- Describe the current common path in plain language.
- Select a safe, representative sample or observation window.
- Record current volume, delay, rework, and manual fallback where relevant.
- Choose a small quality measure tied to the user’s next action.
- Note source, policy, staffing, or tooling conditions that affect the baseline.
- Explain the observation purpose and privacy boundary to participants.

## Required deliverable

Create an **Operating Baseline Record** with the current workflow, sample method, safe evidence references, starting usage and quality signals, known burdens, material conditions, privacy controls, and the date range for comparison.
