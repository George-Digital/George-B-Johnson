# Confirm the system and operating owner

**A system becomes operable when one named person can state its purpose, scope, authority, review boundary, and next decision.**

Before I ask people to adopt an AI-assisted workflow, I make sure we can identify the system we are actually asking them to use. I also name the operating owner who can make routine decisions, receive evidence, and route work that belongs elsewhere.

I have seen capable teams lose momentum because they describe the system as a broad idea rather than a bounded operating process. “Our AI assistant” is not enough of a definition. I want a person to be able to answer: who uses this, for what recurring situation, what approved inputs does it use, what output does it prepare, and what happens after a human reviews it? If those answers change from person to person, adoption will expose the ambiguity rather than solve it.

I write the system definition in one sentence. I name the user, the trigger, the output, and the next action. Then I write what it does not do. This is not pessimism. It prevents an early user from treating a draft as an authorization, a source of truth, or a decision that should remain with a qualified person. In health, legal, financial, employment, safety, disciplinary, eligibility, or access work, I keep the AI-assisted result advisory and make the qualified human’s approval visible.

The operating owner is not necessarily the executive sponsor, the person who first built the prompt, or the most technical person. I choose the person close enough to the work to see failures and authorized enough to keep the common path current. That owner accepts routine questions, confirms the approved version, coordinates small changes, and asks for help when a decision crosses their authority. They do not have to solve every issue alone.

I separate operating ownership from related roles. A source owner decides whether the policy, knowledge base, or reference material is current. A reviewer checks output before a consequential action. A privacy or access owner sets the approved data boundary when needed. A technical maintainer handles a connector or tool configuration. One person may hold several roles in a small team, but I still write the distinctions down. Otherwise a request can sit in a shared inbox while everyone assumes someone else owns it.

I also test whether the owner has what they need. Can they reach the current instructions? Can they see the approved input location, the output standard, the escalation route, and the change record? If they inherit only a clever prompt and a verbal explanation, they cannot operate the process responsibly. I give them a simple home for the operating record and a clear route to pause use when a source, permission, or output is in doubt.

Ownership should be reviewed, not presumed permanent. People change roles, teams reorganize, and a pilot can become a more consequential process than it was at launch. I set a date to confirm the owner again after the pilot. A named owner with limited authority and a clear escalation path is more useful than an impressive title with no operating routine.

## Concrete example

I might help a nonprofit program team use an AI-assisted intake summary for volunteer applications. The system takes an approved application export and prepares a structured summary of stated availability, relevant experience, and missing information. It does not select volunteers or make suitability decisions. The program coordinator is the operating owner, a program manager reviews any recommendation or sensitive concern, and the privacy lead approves the application-data boundary. If the approved form changes, the coordinator pauses the workflow until the source owner confirms the instructions.

## Action checklist

- Write a one-sentence definition for the current system.
- Name the trigger, approved inputs, output, and user’s next action.
- State what the system does not decide or authorize.
- Assign an operating owner and list their routine authority.
- Name the source, review, privacy or access, and technical roles that apply.
- Choose the visible location for the current operating record.

## Required deliverable

Create a **System Ownership Record** with the system definition, scope, exclusions, operating owner, related roles, approved system home, human-review point, privacy boundary, escalation route, and the date you will reconfirm ownership.
