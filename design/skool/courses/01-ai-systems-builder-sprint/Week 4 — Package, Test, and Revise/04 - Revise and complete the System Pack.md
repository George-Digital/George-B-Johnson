# Revise and complete the System Pack

**Complete your System Pack by preserving the evidence from your tests and feedback, making only the revisions that evidence supports, documenting limitations and human-review boundaries, and handing off one current, inspectable version of the system.**

This final step is not a declaration that your system is finished forever. I use it to turn four weeks of choices, drafts, tests, and critique into one honest operating package. A completed System Pack lets another capable person see what the system is for, how to run the common path, what evidence supports the current version, and where a human must still decide.

I start revision with the evidence, not the urge to polish. I review each test record, rubric result, reviewer observation, and unresolved question. Then I make a small list of findings. A finding might be that the output omitted a required field, that a missing-input case did not pause, that an operator could not locate the current source, or that a reviewer could not tell which claim needed verification. I separate those findings from my preferred fixes. This keeps me from adding features that sound useful but do not address what I observed.

For each finding, I decide whether to revise, defer, narrow, or stop. I revise when the problem is inside the current scope and I can identify a bounded change to the prompt, template, runbook, decision rule, example, system home, or review step. I defer when the idea may be useful but needs different permissions, more evidence, or a future version. I narrow when the workflow tried to cover a case that has different rules or a different owner. I stop when the input, tool, evidence, or review arrangement cannot safely support the task. These are all responsible outcomes of a sprint.

I make revisions traceable. I record the version before the change, the evidence that prompted it, what I changed, why I expect it to help, and the test I will use to inspect it. For example, if a draft chose a deadline that was absent from the input, I might add a direct unknown-information instruction and a required “Needs confirmation” field in the template. I do not write “improved prompt” and move on. A specific record lets me rerun the same safe case and see whether the revised behavior changed.

I do not erase the imperfect output that led to the revision. The original test, the reviewer’s result, and the revision note are evidence of how the system was built. When private material is involved, I preserve only a secure approved reference or a sanitized summary in the pack. The visible record should show the learning without exposing a person’s information, confidential source material, credentials, or a decision that belongs in an authorized system.

After the revision, I retest the affected path when practical. I use the same safe case if I want to compare behavior, or a closely related safe case if repeating it would not test the change. I score the new output against the same rubric and record whether the gate now passes, still fails, or exposes a different limitation. A single retest does not guarantee future performance. It gives me a clearer basis for keeping the current version, adding another small test, or restricting its use.

Then I assemble the System Pack so it works as a handoff, not as a scrapbook. I include the system definition and scope; user, input, output, and next action; workflow map; decision rules and minimum SOP; current prompt or workflow instructions; examples and constraints; quality rubric; system home and access boundary; manual test evidence; feedback record; revision history; limitations; and next-improvement list. I make the current version easy to identify, while keeping older versions or references available enough to explain a change.

The final handoff names real people and real limits. I state who can operate the system, who reviews the draft, who owns the source material and updates, where the approved version lives, and what should happen when a required input is absent or sources conflict. I say plainly what the system does not decide. AI may prepare, organize, extract, and draft within approved materials; it does not make final legal, medical, financial, safety, employment, eligibility, disciplinary, or access decisions. The qualified human owner verifies evidence and authorizes the outcome.

I also include a simple demo or orientation when it will help the next operator. The demo should use an authorized or synthetic example and show the common path, the output, the review gate, and the stop condition. I pair it with the live page or template rather than relying on a recording alone. A demo can become outdated; the system home remains the source of truth and carries the version date.

Finally, I write the next smallest improvement. It may be another safe edge-case test, a clearer input field, a better source reference, or a second reviewer’s critique. I name the evidence needed before I do it. This protects the team from treating a promising first version as a reason to automate more quickly than the workflow can be inspected. The System Pack is complete for this sprint when it makes the current system usable, reviewable, and honest about its limits.

## Concrete example

I complete a System Pack for an internal event-brief workflow. Manual testing showed that the draft handled a complete schedule well but buried missing accessibility information. A volunteer lead also said the handoff owner was unclear. I preserve the first output reference and rubric result, revise the template to put “Missing information and owner” directly below the summary, add a handoff field to the runbook, and rerun the incomplete safe case. The revised draft now exposes the gap and routes it to the event lead, but the pack still states that safety planning and accessibility decisions require human review. The final workspace page links the current template, runbook, test record, and a sanitized walkthrough.

## Action checklist

- Review each test result, rubric score, feedback observation, and unresolved question before editing the system.
- Classify every finding as revise, defer, narrow, or stop, and record the reason.
- Make one bounded change at a time to the rule, prompt, template, runbook, system home, or review step.
- Preserve the prior version and a safe or secure reference to the evidence that prompted the change.
- Retest the affected common path or edge case and record the new rubric result.
- Assemble the current system definition, instructions, rubric, examples, tests, feedback, revisions, limitations, and next improvement in one System Pack.
- Name the current location, version, operator, reviewer, update owner, handoff, and stop or escalation route.
- Add a sanitized demo or orientation only if it helps someone follow the current documented process.

## Required deliverable

Complete and save your **Final System Pack**. It must include the current system home, scope, workflow map, decision rules, runbook, prompt or workflow instructions, examples and constraints, quality rubric, at least two manual test records, one feedback record, revision evidence, explicit limitations, human-review and privacy boundaries, final handoff details, and one next-improvement decision with the evidence needed to pursue it.
