# Complete the reliability pack

**I complete the Reliability Pack by assembling the current evidence, boundaries, owners, and operating decisions into one usable record that is honest about what the system can and cannot do.**

I finish this course with a handoff-ready pack that helps an operator run the intended use, a reviewer inspect it, and an owner decide when to improve, restrict, or pause it.

A Reliability Pack is not a certificate and it is not a claim that a system will behave correctly in every future situation. It is the current operating record for one bounded workflow. I use it to make the system inspectable: a capable person can see what is in scope, what evidence has been reviewed, who owns each decision, what fails the gates, where data belongs, and what happens when the normal path breaks.

I begin with the scope note. It names the user, intended use, approved input and source boundary, output, next action, version, and out-of-scope cases. I make the current version easy to find. A pack that mixes an old prompt, a new rubric, and an unknown source date creates the appearance of documentation without a reliable operating reference.

Next, I assemble the evaluation evidence. I include the representative case inventory, expected behavior, edge and adversarial cases, scoring rubric, acceptance criteria, threshold rule, reviewer notes, and safe references to outputs. I show the gates separately from aggregate scores. I also include calibration observations or a note explaining why independent review was not appropriate or available for the current low-risk scope. The pack should make the evidence legible without exposing private case data.

I add the reliability controls: stop and escalation conditions, fallback and recovery path, data and permission map, review and decision ownership, incident and change logs, and evaluation cadence. I check that the names and authorities agree across these documents. If the escalation map says a privacy lead receives an issue but the permissions map gives no one access to the needed record, I have found a real operating gap. Completion means reconciling those connections, not merely filling every template field.

I write the human-review boundary in plain language. The operator knows what they may run. The reviewer knows what evidence they must check. The decision owner knows what they authorize. The system’s advisory role is explicit. For health, legal, employment, compensation, finance, credit, insurance, housing, benefits, education access, safety, discipline, or essential services, the final determination remains with an appropriately qualified human using the required process. The Reliability Pack can support that person’s work; it cannot replace their judgment or responsibility.

I document limitations prominently. A limitation might be that the workflow supports only current English-language intake forms, that it cannot resolve conflicting sources, that it needs manual review of every output, or that a data category is excluded until authorized handling exists. I do not bury those limits in a final note. They are instructions for safe use. I also record the evidence that would be needed before I expand a boundary: more representative tests, a new permission approval, a domain review, stronger source traceability, or a successful fallback exercise.

I complete a handoff check. I ask whether a new authorized operator can find the system home, identify the current version, use the approved inputs, apply the rubric, reach the reviewer, follow a stop condition, and log a failure. I ask whether the decision owner accepts the current scope and cadence. If the answer is no, I treat that as an unfinished artifact, not a reason to declare completion because the files exist.

Then I make one next-improvement decision. I may keep the system in the current limited scope, revise a documented failure, run a new edge test, restrict a risky use, or pause until an owner resolves a blocker. I choose the smallest action supported by the evidence. The goal is not to create endless governance paperwork. The goal is to operate one AI-assisted workflow with enough clarity that people can use, question, and improve it responsibly.

## Concrete example

I complete a Reliability Pack for a volunteer-shift brief workflow. The pack contains eight representative cases, four boundary cases, a five-criterion rubric, a source-grounding gate, a manual fallback, and a monthly review plan. It states that the system may draft a brief from the current approved schedule but cannot make safety assignments or resolve accessibility needs. The event lead reviews every brief and owns escalations. A recent failure about a missing venue instruction remains open until the source owner confirms a fix and a safe retest passes.

## Action checklist

- Assemble the current scope, version, intended use, and out-of-scope boundary.
- Include evaluation cases, expected behavior, scores, gates, thresholds, and safe evidence references.
- Reconcile owners, permissions, escalation, fallback, incident, change, and cadence records.
- Make human-review and high-impact decision boundaries visible.
- List current limitations and the evidence needed before expansion.
- Run a handoff check with an authorized operator or reviewer.
- Record one evidence-based keep, revise, restrict, or pause decision.

## Required deliverable

Complete the **Reliability Pack** using the Reliability Review Template. It must include the scope, evaluation set, rubric and thresholds, failure and escalation log, permissions map, fallback plan, ownership map, incident and change records, cadence, limitations, handoff acceptance, and one next-improvement decision.
