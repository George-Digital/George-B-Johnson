# Week 3 — Operate the Reliability Pack With Named Human Ownership

**Embed on:** `Week 3 — Governance and Operations/01 - Assign review and decision ownership.md`

**Length:** 8–10 minutes

**Outcome:** I will show members how to assemble a living Reliability Pack with named review and decision ownership, connected incident and change records, an evaluation cadence, limitations, and a handoff-ready operating decision. The result is a bounded operating record, not a certificate or a substitute for qualified human judgment.

## What to demonstrate on screen

Use a **synthetic volunteer-shift brief**. It may draft a brief from a fictional approved schedule for event-lead review. It must not make safety assignments, resolve accessibility needs, or make decisions about any person. Use no real volunteer, schedule, health, accessibility, contact, or incident information.

Open these exact local resources in rendered Markdown preview:

1. `Resources/Governance and Permissions Checklist.md` — show **Role and decision map** and **Change and emergency authority**.
2. `Resources/Failure and Escalation Log.md` — show one synthetic near-miss record, its investigation fields, and **Pattern review**.
3. `Resources/Incident and Change Record.md` — show **Incident and change identity**, **Safe evidence, impact, and containment**, **Correction and change plan**, **Test and retest record**, and **Approval, communication, closure, and next review**.
4. `Resources/Reliability Review Template.md` — show **Evidence inventory**, **Evaluation cadence plan**, **Limitations and expansion boundary**, **Handoff and operating check**, and **Current operating decision**.
5. `Resources/Evaluation Set Template.md` — show the synthetic edge-case evidence reference.
6. `Resources/Output Scoring Rubric Template.md` — show the calibration record and threshold reference.

Use only fictional records: `SYN-VS-08` with a missing venue instruction; a connected change labeled `v1.2`; an event-lead reviewer; and a fictional safe retest. Do not show a live incident system, personal schedule, real communications, or sensitive evidence.

## Teleprompter script

[ON SCREEN: A simple title card: “Reliability Pack = current evidence + boundaries + owners + operating decisions.”]

I’m George. This final week turns the work from the first two weeks into an operating practice.

A Reliability Pack is not a certificate. It does not say that an AI system is safe, compliant, fair, or correct in every future case. It is the current record for one bounded workflow: what it is allowed to do, the evidence we have reviewed, the people who own decisions, the conditions that stop the workflow, and the next improvement we have chosen.

[ON SCREEN: Open `Resources/Governance and Permissions Checklist.md` at **Role and decision map**.]

I start with human ownership. “The team reviews it” is not enough. I name an operational owner who keeps the workflow usable; an operator who runs approved inputs; a reviewer who checks the output and evidence; a decision owner who authorizes the next action; an escalation owner; and a change owner. I also name any qualified privacy, security, legal, safety, HR, or domain owner needed for the use.

[ON SCREEN: Highlight authority limits and backup coverage.]

Each role needs a decision statement and a limit. The operator may run the current version and must pause on a defined signal. The reviewer may mark a brief pass, revise, route, or stop, but may not authorize an out-of-scope action. The event lead owns the final use of this synthetic volunteer-shift brief. The change owner approves a revision only after the expected test evidence exists.

I include backup coverage. If the reviewer is absent, I name a qualified alternate or I choose the safe action: wait, use the approved manual path, or stop. I do not solve an absence by giving the AI or an unauthorized person broader decision rights.

[ON SCREEN: Open `Resources/Failure and Escalation Log.md` at a synthetic near-miss entry for `SYN-VS-08`.] 

Next, I keep an incident or near-miss log. Here, the draft omitted a fictional venue instruction that was in an approved schedule note. The event lead caught it before distribution. I record a safe summary, the version, expected and observed behavior, potential impact, containment, the evidence reference, and the owner.

The incident record answers what happened in operation. It does not need to assign blame, and it must not duplicate restricted case data in a broad workspace.

[ON SCREEN: Open `Resources/Incident and Change Record.md` at **Incident and change identity**, then **Safe evidence, impact, and containment**.] 

Now I create the connected record: `SYN-IC-08`, status contained, linked `SYN-VS-08`, current version, expected behavior, and observed omission. I use an approved secure reference and sanitized summary, rather than the real schedule or personal information. I record impact, manual-checklist containment, owner, and any formal reporting route.

[ON SCREEN: Continue in `Resources/Incident and Change Record.md` at **Ownership and decision authority** and **Correction and change plan**.] 

This resource separates what happened from the decision about what to do next. In our synthetic example, the event lead can keep the workflow limited or route it; the change owner proposes `v1.2` to add a required venue-instruction check. I name the correction owner, prior version, expected effect, and non-change boundary: it cannot make safety assignments or resolve accessibility needs.

[ON SCREEN: Continue in `Resources/Incident and Change Record.md` at **Test and retest record**, then **Approval, communication, closure, and next review**.] 

Before limited use resumes, I record the safe incomplete-case test, expected route, reviewer, result, and retest decision. The authorized approver records the current limited scope, notifications, closure evidence, and next review trigger. This is a traceable decision for this version and boundary, not a guarantee of future results.

I do not assume every incident needs a prompt edit. The input template, source, training, fallback, permission rule, or intended scope may be the right place to change. If I defer the work, I record why and who will revisit it in this resource. Keeping the failure log and the Incident and Change Record separate but connected prevents the system from drifting based on memory.

[ON SCREEN: Open `Resources/Reliability Review Template.md` at **Evidence inventory**.]

Now I assemble the Reliability Pack. The evidence inventory makes the current scope note, evaluation set, scoring rubric, thresholds, failure and escalation log, permissions map, fallback plan, ownership map, incident and change records, and cadence visible in one place.

I check that these records agree. If the escalation map names a privacy owner but the permissions map does not let that owner access the appropriate secure evidence, I have found a real gap. Completing the pack means reconciling those connections, not merely filling in template fields.

[ON SCREEN: Briefly show `Resources/Evaluation Set Template.md` edge case and `Resources/Output Scoring Rubric Template.md` calibration record.] 

I also keep the evaluation evidence legible. I show common-path and edge-case coverage, gates, reviewer notes, calibration observations, and limitations. A disagreement between reviewers can show that the rubric needs clarification. A failed adversarial route can show that the escalation path needs repair. Neither result disappears because the average score looks favorable.

[ON SCREEN: Return to `Resources/Reliability Review Template.md` at **Evaluation cadence plan**.] 

Reliability evidence becomes stale. Sources, tools, permissions, people, and real operating conditions change. So I set a scheduled review rhythm and early-review triggers. A weekly review may make sense while a new, low-consequence workflow is stabilizing. A material prompt, tool, source, integration, access, user-group, or threshold change requires an earlier review. Repeated gate failures, reviewer disagreement, a privacy concern, or a missed escalation do too.

At each review, the people with the right authority inspect a safe, representative sample, open incidents, source freshness, calibration, fallback use, and pending changes. They record a decision: keep the current limited scope, revise, restrict, pause, or seek specialized review. A meeting alone is not a cadence; the evidence and decision rights must be clear.

[ON SCREEN: Show **Limitations and expansion boundary** and read a synthetic limitation.] 

I make limitations prominent. This synthetic volunteer-shift workflow may support a draft from the current approved schedule, but it cannot make safety assignments or resolve accessibility needs. It has not been evaluated for a new source format or broader user group. Before expansion, I may need more representative tests, permission approval, a qualified domain review, or a successful fallback exercise.

In any high-impact context involving health, law, employment, compensation, finance, credit, insurance, housing, benefits, education access, safety, discipline, or essential services, the AI remains advisory. A qualified human with the appropriate authority reviews the evidence and makes the final determination. The pack supports accountable work; it never transfers that responsibility to an AI system.

[ON SCREEN: Show **Handoff and operating check** and **Current operating decision**.] 

Finally, I run a handoff check. Can an authorized operator find the current version and approved source? Can they apply the rubric, reach a reviewer, follow the stop and fallback path, and keep restricted evidence in the approved location? Can the decision owner identify what they approve and what they must not delegate?

If the answer is no, the pack is not complete yet. I choose the smallest evidence-based next action. For our synthetic workflow, that might be “keep the current limited draft scope, add a safe retest for the venue-instruction check, and review after the next approved source update.” That is a useful operating decision. It is honest about both the evidence and its limits.

## Member action

1. Complete named roles, authority limits, backup coverage, change approval, and emergency-stop authority in `Resources/Governance and Permissions Checklist.md`.
2. Add one synthetic or safely sanitized near miss to `Resources/Failure and Escalation Log.md`, then open `Resources/Incident and Change Record.md` and complete a connected record with identity, safe evidence, impact, containment, owner, decision authority, correction, approval, safe retest, status, closure, and next review.
3. Assemble the evidence inventory, cadence, limitations, handoff check, and current decision in `Resources/Reliability Review Template.md`.
4. Link the edge-case evidence in `Resources/Evaluation Set Template.md` and the calibration or threshold evidence in `Resources/Output Scoring Rubric Template.md`.
5. Make one documented keep, revise, restrict, or pause decision. Do not expand a high-impact use or remove qualified human final authority based on a score alone.
